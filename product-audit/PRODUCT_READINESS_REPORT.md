# Product Readiness Report

**Product:** raising  
**Audit date:** 2026-10-22  
**Audited revision:** `0e4b36b` (`main`)  
**Verdict:** **NOT READY for production use with real pastoral data**  
**Secondary verdict:** **CONDITIONALLY READY for a controlled POC using synthetic data only**

## Executive decision

raising has a coherent product model and unusually strong foundations for role-scoped pastoral care: member reporting, first-timer follow-up, case management, privacy review, notifications, audit logging, and outcome metrics are connected in the source. The repository is not yet safe to release to real users, however. Current bearer tokens can retain access after deactivation, role change, or an administrator-issued password reset; the production POC does not schedule the hourly safety escalation; and the promised critical email channel is not production-configured or delivery-observable. The repository's own final end-to-end walkthrough is still unchecked, and the audit could not execute any authenticated journey because the test PostgreSQL service was unavailable.

No P0 was confirmed. Three P1 product/operational risks and one P1 verification gate remain open.

## Target stage and confidence

- **Evidence-supported stage:** deployed POC / pre-production candidate. `DEPLOY-POC.md` calls the current deployment a POC and lists work required “before real use.” `PHASE.md` still has production variables, email-domain setup, and the final walkthrough unchecked.
- **Audit confidence:** high for source-level architecture, permissions, and documented deployment gaps; high for the public login surface; low for authenticated runtime behavior and data persistence because no safe database-backed environment was available.
- **Assumption:** “production ready” means use with real congregation and pastoral-care data, not a demonstration with synthetic records.

## Product understanding

### Mission and value proposition

raising replaces scattered email and informal pastoral-care updates with one structured, role-scoped system. Its signature experience is a closed operational loop: a leader records member wellbeing; concern or safety signals become owned cases; the pastor sees risk and silence; follow-up workers progress first-time visitors through contact and conversion; audit and privacy tools preserve trust.

### Business model

No commercial model is documented. The code and roadmap explicitly target a single organisation; multi-tenancy is out of scope.

### Roles and success criteria

| Role | Trigger and goal | Key permissions | Successful outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pastor | Needs a reliable view of care across the ministry | All pastoral records, cases, users, settings, privacy, audit | Risks are seen, assigned, resolved, and auditable |
| Leader | Needs to care for an assigned roster | Own members, their reports, own concern cases | Every assigned member is regularly reported on and concerns progress |
| Follow-up lead | Needs to coordinate first-timer outreach | All first-timers, assignments, calls, conversion | Work is allocated, callbacks happen, interested visitors convert cleanly |
| Follow-up member | Needs to know whom to contact today | Own assignments plus claimable pool | Calls and outcomes are logged without duplicate ownership |
| Superadmin | Needs to support the platform without reading pastoral content | Users, settings, audit, aggregate structure/metrics | Platform can be administered without crossing the pastoral-data boundary |
| Data subjects / external stakeholders | Need sensitive personal information handled responsibly | No account; represented by privacy and retention controls | Data has a basis, can be exported, reviewed, and redacted appropriately |

### Core entities and lifecycle

- `User`: active/inactive, role, password-change gate.
- `Member`: active/inactive, assigned leader and optional group; has reports and cases.
- `MemberReport`: good / needs attention / concern; optional confidential and safety flags; may be redacted or deleted under policy.
- `Case`: concern / safety; open → acknowledged → resolved; owner, plan, due date, resolution note.
- `FirstTimer`: pending → contacted / interested / not interested → converted; optional assignee and call history.
- `Notification`: unread → read, generated by assignments, silence, safety, escalation, retention, and role changes.
- `ActivityLog`: append-only event history.
- `Setting`: string-backed operational policy.

### Documentation contradictions and assumptions

- The original PRD describes a single pastor as top authority, while later superadmin specifications add a support role that may change users and settings and read aggregate groups/metrics. The later design is implemented and treated as authoritative.
- The PRD promises a duplicate-phone warning when adding a member, but the service performs no duplicate lookup and returns no warning.
- The PRD specifies activity-log filters by user, action, and date range; the UI has pagination only, and the API supports user/action but not dates.
- `PHASE.md` labels Phase 7 “Production-ready,” but its production environment, email domain, and end-to-end walkthrough items remain unchecked.

## Critical journey summary

Twenty material journeys were identified. One limited public journey (responsive login-shell interaction) passed. Three journeys were partially inspected through browser/static evidence. Sixteen authenticated/data-changing journeys were blocked or not run. **Zero critical authenticated journeys passed end to end.** See `USER_JOURNEY_MATRIX.md`.

## Strong implementation evidence

- Server-side role guards and service-level row scoping are explicit for members, first-timers, cases, privacy, and audit data.
- Confidential reports are intended to be filtered at query level, not hidden only in the client.
- Concern and safety reports are connected to owned cases with acknowledgement and resolution states.
- First-timer queue policy, callbacks, assignment, claiming, and conversion are modeled as real data transitions.
- Activity logging is append-only at the route surface, and many writes join their domain transaction.
- The public login surface is responsive at 375×667 with no horizontal overflow; modal focus, Escape, and focus restoration worked.
- The full TypeScript/Vite production build passed.

## Highest-risk gaps

1. Existing JWTs are not invalidated or re-authorized after deactivation, role changes, or administrator password reset.
2. Hourly escalation of unacknowledged safety cases is not scheduled in the documented POC deployment.
3. Safety and role-change email uses a placeholder `.local` sender, suppresses delivery failures, and has no retry or observable delivery state.
4. No authenticated journey or backend assertion ran in this audit; all 135 tests stopped at database setup.
5. The public login endpoint has no application-level throttling, lockout, or abuse controls evidenced in the repository.

## Anti-patterns verdict

**Pass with reservations: the interface does not strongly read as generic AI output.** The muted pastoral palette, scripture-led login, role-specific navigation, and restrained card system have a specific product voice. Reservations: authenticated pages rely heavily on repeated rounded cards/tables, many layouts are conventional dashboard grids, and modal use is frequent. These are secondary to the release risks above.

## Recommended decision

Do not onboard real congregations or import real pastoral notes yet. Permit a controlled POC only with synthetic data, named test accounts, no claim of reliable safety escalation, and an explicit test window. Clear the P1 items, provision a disposable database-backed test environment, run the full suite, and complete the role-by-role walkthrough before reassessing production readiness.
